**Findings, in priority order**

1. **[P1] Lookahead offsets can create negative training targets.** `lookahead.py:101`, mirrored in `cantstop_rust/src/lookahead.rs:72`, adds a common vector to unrefined probability vectors without preserving nonnegativity. Preserving the sum is insufficient. This is an actual pipeline failure, not just a theoretical concern about extrapolation. Using the existing normalized `hashed` mock, the third position from `turn_starts(RuleSet.make(3), 8)` gives the exact k=3 root `[0.3097950514, -0.0054631413, 0.6956680900]`; disabling the offset gives valid probabilities. A completed game from the fourth such position, seed 0, takes five turns and produces two invalid lambda=0 training rows, with minimum target `-0.1428335160`. A separate two-player table has 912 invalid stop vectors among 3,061, ranging from -0.421568 to 1.421568. These are valid normalized mock inputs, not claims about the frequency in a trained net.

   The invalid vectors affect backup comparisons and reach the soft cross-entropy unchanged. A negative target weight rewards driving that class's probability toward zero; the corresponding per-row objective is unbounded below. Fix both backends to keep every leaf in the probability simplex, assert finite/nonnegative/unit-sum targets at the training boundary, and add an end-to-end test with more than two players. A bounded correction or simplex projection is a minimum correctness repair; neither establishes that the offset improves play. Disabling the offset is the immediate workaround.

2. **[P2] Training silently evaluates a different lookahead policy.** `train.py:242–246` clears `exact_root` for arena games on the premise that only recorded values change. That premise holds for the plain one-turn solver, but fails with selective lookahead. Refinement selection in `lookahead.py:39–69` depends on reach from the solve root: before-roll and after-roll searches choose different leaves and correction vectors. On `turn_starts(RuleSet.make(2), 1)[0]`, `hashed`, seed 3, k=3, the sampled-root turn takes 11 decisions and the exact-root turn takes two, with different resulting boards. Thus the arena can report strength for a different search than self-play uses. Preserve `exact_root` when k>0, or explicitly separate target generation from the playing search. Update `lookahead_arena.py`'s `--exact` description and add a combination test; existing plain-versus-exact parity does not cover this case.

3. **[P2] The diagnostic samples the lowest random indices instead of a uniform subset.** `value_accuracy.py:58` draws `3*count` indices, sorts the unique indices, and takes the first `count`. With a 10,000-row pool, count 60, PortableRng seed 0, all selected indices are at most 3,897 and their mean percentile is 19.086%, rather than an expected 50%. Because the pool is grouped by game, this overrepresents earlier generated games and concentrates observations in fewer trajectories. It does not necessarily favor early turns within every game or systematically favor one estimator, but it defeats the stated board-sampling design and weakens the reported uncertainty. Sample uniformly without replacement, sorting only after selection. Retain source-game IDs and use a game-cluster bootstrap or sample independent games for confidence estimates.

4. **[P2] Lookahead personas do not have a persona-independent target operator.** `self_play.py:109–113` constructs and refines the biased solver before `_turn_value` removes the bias; Rust does the same in `selfplay.rs:197–209`. Removing the bias redoes the backup, but does not undo the persona-dependent leaf selection or offset. On the same two-player position, exact k=3 produces mover targets 0.7510435305 with bias 0, 0.7514447441 with +0.03, and 0.7507938750 with -0.03. All are from the same board and evaluator. The narrower claim “unbiased backup of this particular refined table” is correct; the broader guarantee that lambda=0 removes persona dependence from the target is not. If the uniform best-play target is the requirement, generate its refinement selection/weights under an unbiased policy, separately from any refinement needed to play the persona. Otherwise document this as an approximation. The proposed k=0 run is unaffected.

**Answers to the twelve focus questions**

1. **Row/turn alignment is correct.** In both backends, one turn value is recorded for each completed turn, and a board is recorded only after a nonterminal turn. Therefore T turns produce T values and T−1 rows; row i is the input board to turn i+1. `winner_slots[i]` was computed using the row's next active player, and its inverse `(winner - winner_slot) % n` is correct. Mixing absolute-seat vectors before rotating is also correct. There is no off-by-one or wrong-player bootstrap in the reviewed paths.

   At lambda=0 the final outcome accumulator is overwritten by the final turn's search value. That is intentional for a search-distillation target: the pre-roll state can still have less than 100% winning probability even when the realized final turn wins. Do not forcibly set that row to the realized winner in exact mode; doing so would reintroduce outcome-conditioned noise.

2. **A ten-iteration window is a reasonable experiment, not a guaranteed optimum.** In steady state it mixes ten teachers, with mean data age 4.5 iterations relative to the newest batch. The key quantity is how much the network changes over those iterations. Exact averaging removes opening-dice variance, not approximation error or the lag from frozen targets. Reanalysis is not a prerequisite for the first trial. Compare window 1 versus 10 with matched training exposure and, separately, matched wall-clock time; otherwise changing replay also changes optimization and compute.

   Lambda=0 can become self-consistent on a narrow distribution while playing poorly: search chooses among approximate leaf values, the network learns its own search targets, and old targets resist new corrections. Persona-generated states broaden coverage, but do not eliminate this risk. Watch frozen-opponent win rates, calibration on independent rollout boards, current exact-backup residuals on a fixed probe set, and prediction drift by replay age. Low replay loss alone cannot distinguish improvement from fitting stale mistakes. This is a practical concern of off-policy bootstrapping with function approximation, not a proof that this implementation diverges; see [van Hasselt et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02648).

   The request understates terminal anchoring: the solver assigns exact winner vectors to every hypothetical winning stop it enumerates, including counterfactual wins in nonfinal observed turns. Anchors are not confined to the last played turn. Conversely, that does not make the overall neural training operator a proven contraction.

3. **Exact and sampled modes have the same intended fixed-point meaning, but not identical finite-network expected targets.** With k=0, non-bust sampled roots average to the corresponding terms of the exact root. On an opening bust, however, exact mode uses the current net's value of the passed-turn board, while sampled mode forwards the next available search return. At lambda=0 the difference on that branch is the bust probability times `(expected forwarded search return - net value of bust board)`. Thus the sampled implementation is effectively variable-depth there. They agree when those continuation estimates are consistent; this is not a pure variance-only transformation throughout training. For lambda>0, bust passthrough also skips a lambda attenuation. This behavior is documented and can be retained, but it should be understood when interpreting sampled-versus-exact experiments.

4. **Keep persona states and unbiased targets for the proposed run.** With k=0 and lambda=0, persona actions affect which boards are visited, while the value of each visited board is backed up under unbiased play. This is the right separation for learning a generally strong value function from broader coverage. Excluding persona turns would discard the positions the exploration is intended to supply. With lambda>0, the recursion incorporates continuations chosen by personas, so it is no longer a pure best-play target; that is an explicit policy mixture, not merely more label noise. With k>0, finding 4 adds another dependence.

   A conservative opponent may make different future choices from those assumed by this value function. Broader state coverage can help against humans without optimizing exploitation of their mistakes. To establish that benefit, measure balanced-seat matches against fixed conservative and aggressive policies as well as strong ordinary opponents. Opponent-conditioned values or search would be needed to represent those policies explicitly.

5. **The masked soft loss is correct for valid distributions.** The first `masked_fill` makes absent logits constants, so their derivatives are zero; the second removes their contribution to the loss. A direct backward probe returned exactly zero gradients in both absent slots. Shared hidden features still learn from live-seat outputs, as intended. The defect is invalid lookahead targets entering an otherwise correct loss, not leakage through masked logits.

6. **The offset is an unvalidated heuristic even after fixing its bounds.** The selected leaves are chosen using the same approximate values that control their reach. Their errors are not a random representative sample of errors at unrefined leaves. One common correction can therefore miscalibrate distant boards or reverse comparisons with exact terminal values. Reach weights also count visits to rejected stop decisions; they are not mutually exclusive final-outcome probabilities. This prevents interpreting the offset as a general unbiased correction.

   Park offset-enabled lookahead for the next training run. For later experiments, begin with valid probabilities and compare no offset, bounded/damped correction, and a small amount of randomized leaf coverage. Spend further effort only if balanced-seat arena tests show enough strength gain per unit time. A lower rollout RMSE on 60 selected boards is insufficient to justify its measured cost.

7. **Decision reach is defensible, but incomplete.** A rejected stop can become optimal after refinement, so ranking only stops currently taken would miss useful alternatives. Reach alone nevertheless wastes work on decisions with large margins and gives zero priority to branches rejected earlier by a possibly wrong value. A better experiment is reach combined with decision sensitivity or uncertainty, plus coverage of plausible alternative move branches. Pure inverse-margin ranking is also insufficient: a high-reach mistake with a large estimated margin can still be a large real error. Evaluate these choices by action changes and playing strength, not only average board MSE.

8. **Yes, budget live tables separately.** The documented memory limitation is real and is not an additional undisclosed finding. The current row budget is checked after tables are allocated and even admits one oversized game's complete group of blocks, so it is a soft batch limit. Lowering in-flight games is the current practical control. A stronger design admits refinement work under a node/byte budget, evaluates only a bounded number of refiners at once, retains their small root vectors, and releases each table before admitting the next. Account for retained main tables and menu caches as well as feature buffers. A scheduler that budgets only the next network batch cannot enforce a process-memory limit.

9. **Weights-only initialization is an acceptable warm start, not an exact resume.** It resets Adam moments, step count, replay, RNG progression, and schedule numbering. Be explicit about the new learning rate; omitting it restores the 1e-3 default, even when the source checkpoint came from a 1e-4 run. I would not add a large minimum step count just to erase the intentional early ramp: with an empty buffer, that repeatedly fits very little fresh data. Preserve the current gentle start, or restore optimizer/replay state if true continuation is required.

   The lifetime-pass identity applies to rows surviving the full window, approximately after batch rounding. A hard row cap can evict rows sooner, and rows near the end of the run have not yet received their full lifetime exposure. These are operational qualifications, not defects in the stated full-lifetime calculation.

10. **Writing Adam's group learning rate is fine.** It does not reset or invalidate Adam's bias correction; the optimizer step counter and moments keep progressing. A sudden rate change can still change dynamics, and a weights-only restart resets that state by construction. The schedule uses local run iterations. No new scheduler correctness bug found.

11. **The noise correction is right; the truth definition limits the conclusion.** For R independent rollout outcomes, `E[p_hat(1-p_hat)/(R-1)] = p(1-p)/R`, exactly the variance term contaminating squared error against p_hat. Require R>1. The correction cancels from paired estimator differences. Corrected MSE can be negative in a finite sample; clipping only for a displayed square root is reasonable, but the resulting RMSE is not itself unbiased.

   Rollouts define value under the fixed plain-search policy, not optimal value or value under repeated k-turn search. When lookahead changes decisions, its estimate can move toward a different continuation policy, so lower error against plain rollouts is neither necessary nor sufficient for stronger play. The arithmetic is useful for calibration against that named policy after fixing the sampler. For search investment decisions, use direct head-to-head matches and, where useful, policy-matched rollouts. Use game-cluster uncertainty for related source boards; the current per-board SE ignores that dependence.

12. **The capacity result supports a narrow decision only.** Splitting by game is appropriate. Equal held-out losses support “no demonstrated benefit from a larger net for this frozen dataset, training procedure, and validation objective.” They do not prove that capacity is irrelevant to future exact-target training or action quality. Larger nets fitting noisy targets and then overfitting can hide capacity benefits that require more data or different optimization. The cross-entropy includes target entropy; report `CE - H(target)` alongside CE to expose the remaining fitting gap. Also inspect decision-sensitive/late-game errors, multiple initializations, and direct strength after comparable training. Keeping the current network size for the next trial is sensible; treating 0.520 as a universal capacity ceiling is not.

   The comparison to PureTD should also remain qualified: its described training rounds use fresh self-play data followed by one training epoch, whereas this recipe uses multi-iteration replay and repeated lifetime exposure. Exact backup is a useful shared idea, not evidence that all these training settings transfer. [PureTD, section 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15146v1#S3.SS2).
