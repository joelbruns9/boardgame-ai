## 2. Correctness review: estimator, admission, and backup

### 2.1 The estimator is unbiased; renormalization concerns do not apply here

At both deck 8 and deck 12 every unordered four-tile row is equally likely
(`1/70`, `1/495`). The active set is a prefix of a frozen, value-independent
random permutation of the support, so the plain mean over active rows is
unbiased for the chance expectation at every *predetermined* width, not only
at full support. Two second-order caveats (second review) are acknowledged
without changing the estimator: realized width is an adaptive stopping
quantity correlated with the search trajectory, and rows of unequal maturity
mix raw-network and searched values whose systematic errors differ — so the
deployed estimate is near-unbiased rather than exactly unbiased. Equal
probability weighting remains correct; reweighting by maturity or visits is
not permitted. The A1b Hájek-vs-sampled concern was about missing-mass
renormalization under partial coverage of a *lazy* support; with a uniform
support and a value-independent active set, the "renormalized" estimator and
the direct mean are the same thing. No Hájek machinery is needed.

Confirmed invariants from the brief, unchanged:

- Row selection occurs only after PUCT commits the pre-reveal action.
- Row search visits never become probability weights; routing among active
  rows is least-real-visits-first (local balance), which controls coverage
  without touching probabilities.
- Admission/widening rows are charged to the move's NN budget and create zero
  MCTS visits and zero policy-target mass.
- One completed outer simulation adds exactly one parent visit, regardless of
  how many rows its admission group evaluated.

### 2.2 Amendment 1 — delayed admission (`N_init`), not first-crossing admission

Section 8.2 of the brief admits `W0` rows the moment PUCT first commits a
reveal-triggering action. That is too expensive at deep selection roots: a
single 4,800-simulation deck-8 search has been measured materializing roughly
350 chance nodes (24,710 registered outcomes / 70 per node). First-crossing
admission at `W0=4` would spend ~1,400 rows — about 29% of the move budget —
mostly on fringe nodes visited once, violating the preregistered 25%
initialization guardrail before the search does anything useful.

Keep the A1c mechanism that already exists and measured 0.3–2% initialization
overhead: a chance node's first `N_init` crossings run in lazy sampled mode
(one schedule row per crossing, realized-value backup). When real visits reach
`N_init`, the node atomically admits the remainder of its first `W0` schedule
rows in one batched group and switches to the direct active-set mean. Because
lazy traversal follows the same frozen schedule, the already-visited rows are
exactly the schedule prefix; admission just completes the active set.

`N_init=2` is recommended: principal actions convert almost immediately, while
one-visit fringe nodes never pay for a panel.

### 2.3 Amendment 2 — uniform without-replacement schedule, not tile-balanced cycles

The node-local frozen schedule should be a seeded uniform permutation of the
support (rows drawn without replacement), with width measured in rows. Reasons:

- The exact-tail resampling study found matched-width IID at least as good as
  tile-balanced panels at every exposure; balance has earned no advantage.
- Cycle-aligned widths are deck-dependent (a deck-12 cycle is 3 rows), which
  makes one common `4→8→16→32` schedule impossible to state cleanly.
- Without-replacement sampling already guarantees no duplicate rows and keeps
  the subset mean unbiased.

The existing balanced-cycle code paths remain as ablations; they are not the
production schedule.

### 2.4 Amendment 3 — back up the current chance mean, and read parent Q dynamically

On every simulation that crosses an admitted chance node: first update the
traversed row's conditional subtree, then back the node's **current**
active-set mean (not the single realized row return) to ancestors. Pre-admission
lazy crossings back the realized row value, as today. At the admission event,
the triggering simulation backs the fresh `W0`-mean once.

This directly answers brief question 4: stale shallow returns are diluted at
rate `1/N` because every later traversal re-injects the up-to-date mean, and
the corrected A1b path already reconstructs root-child Q from the current
chance estimate rather than accumulated snapshots — keep that. Full
re-propagation of ancestor averages on every panel update is not justified;
at 4,800 simulations the `1/N` wash-out is fast for any action that matters.

The widening gate (brief question 5): widen from width `W` to the next
scheduled width only when

```text
min(real_conditional_visits[row] for row in active_rows) >= D_min
AND remaining_move_budget >= (new_width - W) rows
```

The min-visit form is the simplest gate that cannot strand an undersearched
row, and the hard-budget clause reuses the A1c no-overshoot accounting.
Quantile or total-visit gates add tuning surface without addressing a measured
failure; do not build them for this cycle.

## 3. Admission-depth design: persistent widening, no nested macro-search

Choose the **persistent** design. The nested alternative (each outer selection
runs `D0` inner simulations per admitted row before returning one averaged
result) is rejected for this cycle because:

1. **The evidence says topology, not breadth-at-admission, is the mechanism.**
   Sampled-to-panel TV was 0.015; one-shot breadth added almost nothing. The
   value of conditional depth arrives through repeated visits, which persistent
   widening provides wherever PUCT actually allocates visits.
2. **Its cost lands exactly where A1c warned.** `W0×D0` inner simulations per
   outer visit multiply spend at the chance boundary; the A1c screen showed
   more chance-side work (`X=8`) making search *worse* at fixed budget.
3. **The engineering risk is concentrated in `BatchedMCTS`'s hardest code.**
   Nested budgets, multi-wave atomic completion, and inner-visit isolation
   from outer policy counts are precisely the invariants (Phase V items 4, 5,
   10, 12) that are already hard to test in the persistent design.
4. **The failure mode persistent supposedly has is mitigated twice.** A parent
   depressed by an unlucky mini-panel is protected by the 4-row mean (4× lower
   variance than one row) and by PUCT exploration; and if the 4-row mean is
   genuinely poor, suppressing that action is correct behavior, not a bug.

Skip the "one guaranteed conditional descent per admitted row" compromise too:
it reintroduces multi-wave admission groups for a benefit that `D_min` gating
already delivers on any row PUCT revisits.

**Clarification (owner review, 2026-08-11):** rejecting nested macro-search
does not reject multi-row evaluation at a crossing. The persistent design
already (a) batch-evaluates all `W0` rows at admission and backs their mean up
once, and (b) backs the current mean over *all* active rows on every later
crossing (section 2.4), so every simulation that touches the node uses the
full active panel's information. Because network evaluations are deterministic
and cached, re-evaluating the same rows on later crossings would buy nothing;
what improves estimates after admission is conditional *search*, which
proceeds one descent per simulation in either design. The only thing nested
would add is front-loading `W0×D0` inner descents at admission time — the same
total deepening work persistent widening lets PUCT allocate to the rows and
actions that prove to matter.

A stronger variant was also considered and rejected: advance *all* active rows
one descent on *every* crossing, charged as a single simulation. Charging W
descents as one sim is an accounting relabel — NN rows and wall time are the
real budget — and because ~90% of simulations cross some chance node, the
variant multiplies treated-search cost by roughly W, with the spend
concentrated on the ~hundreds of fringe chance nodes PUCT visits once
(exactly the waste delayed admission exists to avoid). On the principal line
it changes nothing: least-visited rotation already advances every row once
per W crossings, in adaptive rather than forced chunks.

### 4.1 Deck 8

| Init guardrail | ≤25% of move NN budget, hard | Existing A1c accounting, unchanged |

Widening arithmetic (corrected per second review — the v1 text conflated
activation with maturity): with least-visited routing and `D_min=4`, each
width *activates* once every current row has four real visits — width 8 at
~16 crossings, 16 at ~32, 32 at ~64, 64 at ~128, 70 at ~256 — while *mature*
width 70 (every row of the full support searched to `D_min`) arrives at ~280.
Every widening event necessarily injects its new rows at bootstrap-only
maturity (~half the active set at each doubling), so telemetry must record
**active width** (rows in the mean) and **mature width** (widest prefix in
which every row has ≥ `D_min` real visits) as separate fields. Only principal
actions' chance nodes climb the ladder at 4,800 simulations; fringe actions
stay at `W0`. That is the intended width/depth behavior, and it is why a
global static `X` failed while this should not.

Note the ladder does not contradict the exhaustive-panel null: what failed
there was *unearned* breadth — 70 depth-zero rows charged at first crossing.
Ladder width is purchased with depth already banked. The open question is
only whether the top of the ladder is worth anything: capping deck 8 at 16
concentrates a principal node's ~2,000 crossings into ~125 visits/row
instead of ~28, trading a negligible sampling-error term for 4× conditional
depth, and static 16-row panels (A1c `X=8`) regressed while 8-row panels
were the best arm. G3's cap-16 arm settles this against the exact oracle; if
the cap ships, deck 8 and deck 12 share one identical rule.

### 4.2 Deck 12

Identical rule (`N_init=2`, `W0=4`, `D_min=4`), width capped at **16**.

At deck 12, 16 rows is per-tile exposure `X = W·4/n ≈ 5.3` — inside the
productive `X=4–8` band from the exact-tail study, and far from the
fragmentation regime. Coverage of 495 rows is not the goal and is not needed:
the estimator is unbiased at any width; what width buys is variance reduction,
which is nearly exhausted by ~16 uniform rows, and what width costs is
conditional depth, which is the measured failure mode.

### 4.3 Deck-12 scope decision and fallback

**Include deck 12** (the brief's broader option), because:

- it roughly doubles the natural treated share (~4.5% → ~9% of replay), and at
  the mandated 1.0× weight the treated signal is already sparse;
- the pilot proved the deck-12 boundary is mechanically healthy (tens of
  thousands of crossed paths per four games, balanced slots 0–3, zero
  bootstrap rows); and
- the two risks that made deck 12 an extrapolation — no forced-move validation
  and a 495-row support — are addressed by the cap (depth preserved) and by
  the fact that the treatment shares one mechanism whose deck-8 validation is
  the load-bearing evidence.

**Preregistered fallback (mechanical, not strength-based):** if the CUDA smoke
or the first two Phase-B cloud iterations show, at deck-12 treated roots, the
most-visited chance node's median active width stuck at `W0` with `D_min`
unmet, or the 25% init guardrail tripping disproportionately at deck 12, set
the treatment to deck 8 only at the next iteration boundary and continue the
run. Log the switch; do not stop the run and do not consult any strength
metric when deciding.

## 7. Frozen full-cycle configuration

### 7.1 Replay strategy — answer to brief questions 6–8

No stored warm buffer is used. The June/July buffers predate or diverge from
the current regime and carry an audit burden; the pilot buffer contains
2×-weighted treated targets from a drifting generator. The owner's instinct is
correct, with one refinement that recovers most of the warm-buffer benefit:

**Phase A generates its buffer from unchanged `current_best` itself**
(`--selfplay_generator_mode current_best`), while the learner warm-starts from
the same weights and trains on that data. This makes Phase A a controlled
rebuild of exactly the mature incumbent distribution — the "audited compatible
incumbent buffer" the brief prefers, constructed fresh on the box — and it
removes the fresh-buffer-plus-drifting-generator interaction that the matched
control implicates. The learner cannot damage its own data supply during
burn-in because it does not generate anything yet.

Phase B then switches the generator to the (soft-gated) learner and enables
the chance treatment, at natural `1.0×` chance replay weight. The 2× weighting
stays off for the whole cycle; revisit only in a later cycle, after a stable
buffer exists, as its own change.

Writing counterfactual post-reveal search targets to replay remains disabled.

### 7.2 Phase A — replay stabilization (cloud, ~8 iterations)

- Warm-start learner from `current_best`; generator mode `current_best`.
- 8 iterations × 400 games / 300 optimizer steps; incumbent everything:
  4,800/200 playout-cap schedule, 25% full-search recording, lr 1e-4, 80×6,
  batch 256, buffer 200k, exact endgame 3.0 s with `--async_solve`.
- Fresh buffer, `--buffer_autosave_every 1`, all checkpoints kept.
- Chance flags **off**. Expected buffer at end: ~70k examples (~22
  recorded/game).
- Promotion checks do **not** run in `current_best` generator mode — the
  loop gates them to `strict_gate`/`soft_gate` (`self_play.py:4203`, verified
  during second review) — so Phase-A health uses explicit
  **measurement-only** paired evaluations of `iter_0005` and `iter_0008`
  against `current_best` (192 pairs / 384 games, 400 simulations, shared
  open-loop search), run between iterations on the box via the standalone
  match harness. They never alter the generator, any checkpoint, or the
  run-local best. Expected reading ≈50% (the learner is a near-clone).

## 8. Evaluation and promotion design — answer to brief question 9

Two layers reconcile the owner's rolling-gate practice with the iteration-2
lesson:

1. **Rolling soft gate (steers the run).** Every 5 iterations, 192 paired
   seeds / 384 games at 400 simulations, shared open-loop search, against the
   **run-local** best copy (`--current_best_path` pointed inside the run
   directory, seeded from `current_best`). The in-loop rule is the existing
   soft-gate implementation stated in full (second-review clarification):
   run-local promotion requires raw win rate ≥ 55% **and** pair-clustered
   LCB > 50% **and** fixed-suite exact-value MAE within tolerance (0.05).
   The fixed suite is a **veto only**, never promotion evidence, and is not
   disabled. Raw win rate < 48% reverts the generator to the run-local best.
   The global `current_best.pt` is never touched by this loop.
2. **Final confirmation (the only real promotion).** Preregistered before
   launch: candidate = the run-local best at end of Phase B (or the final
   checkpoint if nothing ever cleared the rolling gate — in which case expect
   failure and treat the run as negative); three fresh reserved seed blocks,
   one per gate (e.g. bases 20330000 / 20340000 / 20350000, disjoint from the
   20310000 / 20320000 history); each match 256 pairs / 512 games at 400
   simulations, pair-score LCB > 50%, no optional stopping. The ladder
   (restructured per second review — the v1 rule could have replaced the
   deployed advisor's network without evidence under its deployed search):
   - **Gate 1 (training verdict):** candidate vs unchanged `current_best`,
     both under the reviewed chance-aware search. Fail → no promotion; the
     run is negative. Pass → continue.
   - **Gate 2 (system verdict):** candidate + new search vs `current_best` +
     incumbent open-loop search. Pass → promote the complete system: replace
     `current_best.pt` and adopt chance-aware search for the advisor, with a
     short advisor-budget sanity match recommended before the first Arena
     game (400-simulation evidence extrapolates to the 10,000-simulation
     advisor).
   - **Gate 3 (deployability fallback, run only if Gate 2 fails):** candidate
     vs `current_best`, both under incumbent open-loop search. Pass → promote
     the network under unchanged open-loop deployment. Fail → do **not**
     replace `current_best.pt`; bank the candidate as `best_chance_aware.pt`
     with its Gate-1 artifact — a chance-conditional improvement that is not
     yet deployable informs the next cycle rather than the deployed system.
   The owner runs any promote step manually after reviewing the artifacts.

   Definition **ratified by owner (2026-08-11)**: `current_best.pt` is
   simply the strongest model — a challenger becomes current_best only by
   beating the incumbent in this preregistered head-to-head ladder; how it
   was trained is irrelevant. Because `current_best.pt` doubles as the
   advisor default, "beating the incumbent" must be demonstrated under a
   deployable configuration (Gate 2 or Gate 3); a candidate that cannot is
   banked, not promoted.

The rolling gate's repeated looks cannot cause a false global promotion
because only the reserved-block confirmation promotes; the iteration-2
selection error (rank checkpoints on small screens, then confirm the winner)
is structurally excluded because the candidate rule is fixed before launch.

## 10. Stop conditions

Preregistered; mechanical ones consult no strength metric.

1. **Pre-rental:** any G1–G3 failure → do not rent; fix or stand down.
2. **First hour:** G4 throughput < 0.20 games/s after calibration → destroy
   the box; reassess locally.
3. **Phase A health:** accounting violations (nonzero fake visits, guardrail
   overshoot, budget mismatch) → stop, fix, restart Phase A. If the
   iteration-5 or iteration-8 measurement-only evaluation (section 7.2) reads
   a *confirmed* regression (pair UCB < 50%)
   for what should be a near-clone → stop; the recipe, not chance search, is
   broken (this is the matched-control scenario and must not proceed to
   Phase B).
4. **Phase B mechanical:** deck-12 fallback per section 4.3 (continue at deck
   8); if deck-8 width/depth is *also* unhealthy (median active width stuck at
   `W0` at the most-visited nodes) → stop the run; the mechanism is not
   operating.
5. **Phase B trajectory (owner's early-value heuristic, calibrated):** the
   expected shape is a burn-in-free start (Phase A absorbed it) with the
   absorption diagnostic moving within ~10 iterations, as in the pilot. If by
   Phase B iteration 15 no rolling gate has reached even probation (≥48%)
   **and** the absorption diagnostic is flat or reversing → kill the box,
   post-mortem locally. Do not kill during Phase A on absolute level; Phase A
   is expected to hover at ≈50%, and the informative slope starts in Phase B.
6. **Generator protection:** two consecutive soft-gate reversions in Phase B →
   pause self-play, snapshot, and assess before spending further; a learner
   twice reverted is not converting targets into strength.
7. **Spend/duration:** the vast.ai guardrail is a hard stop; if it fires
   mid-phase, the atomic per-iteration saves make the last completed iteration
   the result.
8. **Data safety:** if off-box sync has failed for more than one iteration,
   pause the run until restored.

## 11. Direct answers to the brief's section-13 questions

1. **`W0=4`, `D_min=4`?** Yes, with `N_init=2` delayed admission; width in
   rows from a uniform without-replacement schedule, not tile-balanced cycles
   (section 2.3). `D_min=8` decided by the G3 screen.
2. **Bootstrap-only vs guaranteed inner work?** Bootstrap-only with persistent
   deepening (section 3).
3. **Direct active-row mean sufficient?** Yes — uniform support makes the
   subset mean unbiased at predetermined widths; adaptive stopping and mixed
   row maturity can introduce second-order systematic error, but neither is
   corrected by visit-weighting (section 2.1).
4. **Ancestor Q vs stale returns?** Back up the current chance mean on every
   traversal; read the parent edge dynamically where already implemented; no
   full re-propagation (section 2.4).
5. **Widening gate?** Min real conditional visits ≥ `D_min` across active
   rows, plus the hard budget clause (section 2.4).
6. **Deck 12?** Include, width cap 16, mechanical fallback (section 4.3).
7. **1× replay frequency?** Yes for the whole cycle; any weighting change is a
   later, separate experiment (section 7.1).
8. **Open-loop stabilization vs gating/warm replay?** Both, combined: Phase A
   *is* generator gating (generator = `current_best`) and produces the warm
   replay in place (section 7.1).
9. **Fixed final checkpoint vs sequential design?** Two-layer design: rolling
   run-local soft gate for steering, and a preregistered reserved-block gate
   ladder (Gates 1–3) with a deployability requirement for the real promotion
   (section 8).
10. **Missing invariants?** Added tests 16–19 (section 6.1): schedule
    permutation-prefix integrity, deck-12 cap enforcement, lossless `N_init`
    conversion, and bit-identical legacy modes.

## 12. What this cycle deliberately does not test

One cycle, one treatment. Excluded, regardless of intermediate results:
counterfactual replay writing, 2× chance weighting, later-reveal splits (A4),
Gumbel roots, capacity changes, BGA restarts, and any blending of previously
null mechanisms. A negative result here is a verdict on the staged
chance-aware recipe, not an invitation to combine it with another lever
mid-run.
